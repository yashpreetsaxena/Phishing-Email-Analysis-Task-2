# Phishing Email Analysis

## Overview

This repository contains my submission for Task 2 of the Cyber Security Internship. The task involved analyzing a phishing email sample and identifying the indicators that suggest the email is fraudulent.

## Objective

The purpose of this task was to understand how phishing emails work and learn how to identify common signs of email-based cyber attacks. By examining a suspicious email, I gained practical experience in recognizing phishing attempts and social engineering techniques.

## Email Sample Analyzed

The email claimed that the recipient was eligible for a tax refund of **209.27 GBP** and appeared to be sent by **HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC)**. The message included a link for claiming the refund and used official-looking branding to appear trustworthy.

## Analysis Performed

### Sender Verification

The email was sent from **[chugo23_5@hotmail.co.uk](mailto:chugo23_5@hotmail.co.uk)**, which is a personal email address and does not belong to HMRC. This is a strong indication that the sender is impersonating a legitimate organization.

### Suspicious Refund Offer

The email informed the recipient that they were entitled to receive a tax refund. Financial rewards are commonly used by attackers to attract attention and persuade users to click malicious links.

### Impersonation of a Trusted Organization

The email used the HMRC name and logo to create a false sense of legitimacy. Attackers often impersonate trusted organizations to gain the victim's confidence.

### Suspicious Hyperlink

The email contained a "Refund" link that encouraged the user to submit a refund request. Such links often redirect victims to fake websites designed to steal personal or banking information.

### Social Engineering Tactics

The email attempted to manipulate the recipient by offering money and encouraging immediate action. This is a common social engineering technique used in phishing attacks.

## Phishing Indicators Identified

* Suspicious sender email address
* Impersonation of a government organization
* Financial incentive to attract victims
* Presence of a suspicious hyperlink
* Use of social engineering techniques
* Misleading and deceptive content

## Key Concepts Learned

* Phishing
* Email Spoofing
* Social Engineering
* Email Security
* Threat Detection
* Cyber Awareness

## Conclusion

After analyzing the email, I concluded that it is a phishing attempt. The sender's email address, fake tax refund offer, and use of a suspicious link are clear warning signs. This task helped me better understand how attackers use phishing emails to trick users into revealing sensitive information and highlighted the importance of verifying email authenticity before taking any action.
